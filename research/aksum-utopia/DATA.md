# Aksum, Built — research and data inventory

Working notes for a "what if" utopia map. The question is: what if Dʿmt and Aksum had built as high as their own construction could carry? Gathered on 2026-11-09.

Confidence ratings:
- **A**: peer-reviewed paper or excavation report.
- **B**: an encyclopedic or institutional source (UNESCO, a museum, Wikipedia citing scholarship).
- **C**: tourism or popular sources. Check these before printing.

---

## 1. The anchor: the "16–19 storeys" finding

**Grat Beʿal Gebri palace, Yeha** (Dʿmt / pre-Aksumite period, c. 800 BC). It sits about 200 m north-east of the Great Temple.

| Fact | Value | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date | c. 800 BC, destroyed by fire in antiquity | BTU press release 2026; Drieschner & Schnelle 2026 | A |
| Footprint | "about 60 × 60 m" (press); "around 60 m × 50 m" (2023 paper); FE model 47.52 × 37.44 m for storeys 1–5 | BTU 2026; Drieschner et al. 2023 | A |
| Podium | 6 m high | Drieschner et al. 2023 | A |
| Walls | Rubble masonry in clay mortar with horizontal timber beams, alternating direction. 1.90 m above the podium, 2.20 m in the podium | Drieschner et al. 2023 | A |
| Storey height used in model | 3.36 m clear height + 0.96 m ceiling = **4.32 m per storey** | Drieschner et al. 2023 | A |
| Reconstruction | 5 standard storeys + 3 recessed = **8 storeys** above the podium | Drieschner et al. 2023; BTU 2026 | A |
| Structural limit | "a structure with **at least 19 storeys** would have been structurally feasible" (BTU). Press coverage also gives 16. | BTU 2026; Heritage 9(7):270 | A |
| Entrance | Propylon with six monolithic pillars, 0.85 × 0.95 × 10.10 m | Drieschner et al. 2023 | A |
| Evidence of upper floors | Stair tower | Drieschner et al. 2023 | A |
| Timber | African olive and *Cordia africana* | Greek Reporter (from the study) | B |

**Caveat to print on the map:** the experts quoted in the coverage (Tanasi, Konadu) stress that 19 is a structural capacity, not a height the palace reached. "A wall carrying 19 floors is not the same as a structure that stood 19 floors high." This caveat is the premise of the utopia.

**Derived heights** (my arithmetic, using the paper's 6 m podium and 4.32 m storey):
- 8 storeys (the reconstruction): about 41 m.
- 16 storeys: about 75 m.
- 19 storeys (the structural limit): about **88 m**.

**Regional parallel:** Arab historians describe South Arabian palaces of up to 20 floors, for example Ghumdan in Sanaʿa (Newsweek's coverage of the study). Rock and wall paintings show buildings with "a much higher number of stories" (Drieschner et al. 2023). **B**

## 3. Aksum: the stelae (the "carved skyscrapers")

| Stele | Height | Storeys carved | Weight | Status | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, Great Stele | 33 m | 13 | about 520 t | fallen and broken | B / C (weight) |
| 2, Rome Stele | 24.6 m | 10 (my count from a photo) | about 160 t | returned 2005, re-erected 2008 | B |
| 3, King Ezana's Stele | 20.6 m | 9 (my count from a photo) | about 160 t | standing | B |

Other stelae measure 18.2, 15.8 and 15.3 m (Wikipedia, "Aksum"). The carved storeys average about 2.5 m each, so the stelae show buildings at human scale, not stretched ones.

## 6. Life in the city (for the lifestyle panel)

- **Trade:** a Red Sea trading power via the port of Adulis (Pleiades: Adouli, 15.2616 N, 39.6591 E), between Rome and India. The *Periplus of the Erythraean Sea* names Aksum.
- **Coins:** gold, silver and bronze, from about 270 to the early 600s.
- **Writing:** Geʿez.
- **Religion:** a crescent-and-disc religion first; Christianity from Ezana onward.
- **Housing:** most people lived among fields and pastures around the monumental core (Sulas).

## 9. The map these numbers support

**"Aksum, Built":** a bird's-eye view of the real terrain of Aksum (Copernicus heights, OSM sites). Every real site gets the building its own engineering allowed:
- towers of up to **19 storeys / about 88 m** (the Yeha wall limit);
- in the Aksumite style the stelae record, about 2.5–4.3 m per storey;
- an inset of Yeha as the foundation;
- a timeline from Dʿmt to the end of the gold coinage;
- a lifestyle panel.

Real sites are drawn in ink, imagined buildings in a second colour. Footer: *"Every place is real. Every tower is imagined. Every height is one their walls could carry."*
