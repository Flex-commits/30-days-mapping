## Concept decision — The Stitch

The preferred circle is not the legal boundary. Choose **The Stitch** instead: real shorelines make a seam; selected agency crossings form gold stitches across it. Selected airports and Newark/Elizabeth docks extend the story beyond the river. This is a north-up geographic map with schematic crossing links, not a network alignment survey or full inventory.

Gold is reserved for agency-associated infrastructure. Coastlines remain quiet. Six tiny eyes locate the existing viewing places, tying the chapter to Sight without claiming computed visibility. Water lettering follows a curve; state names use spaced capitals. **1921** is the dominant expressive-type anchor; selected years describe different, clearly named events. Other scholarly context and facility totals belong in short cited web text.

## Composition passes

1. **Pass 1 — landscape proof:** replaced the aerial/statistical sheet with a dominant shoreline seam, a left-hand 1921 anchor and five small milestone groups. Inspection exposed crowded upper-right state/bridge lettering and overly heavy rectangular land fills. The airport CSV also exposed its latitude/longitude field order; this was corrected before validation or publication.
2. **Pass 2 — less ink:** removed land-block borders and retained true coastlines only, moved the New York label, added stroke/symbol keys and separated the airport/dock anchors. The portrait edition needed a shorter water label to fit its curve; the renderer correctly rejected the oversized first attempt.
3. **Pass 3 — phone and identity:** named the Port Authority explicitly, enlarged phone labels, separated tunnel labels, used HUDSON on the shorter mobile curve, and placed the harbor label in the lower harbor rather than toward Jamaica Bay. Kept dates outside the mobile map and in readable page text. Final label bounds and visual inspections are recorded separately.

No pass was pushed. The original poster, pair sheets and square gallery thumbnail retain their baseline bytes.


## Source geometry and coordinate audit

**Pass 4 (geography):** Natural Earth 1:10m was too coarse at this scale (Manhattan read as a wedge; crossings did not meet the banks), so it was replaced. Land: U.S. Census 2024 cartographic boundary counties, 1:500k, https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/GENZ2024/shp/cb_2024_us_county_500k.zip , minus TIGER/Line 2024 area-water polygons (≥5 ha) for the 13 counties in frame, https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/TIGER2024/AREAWATER/ . State line: shared NY–NJ boundary of TIGER/Line 2024 states, https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/TIGER2024/STATE/tl_2024_us_state.zip . All public domain; hashes in `data/port-authority-sources.json`. Crossing anchors are snapped to their own state's shoreline (snap distances recorded, all under 600 m).

**Pass 4 (design):** both states share one land colour so the dashed state line is the only divider; years in IM FELL sit on the map beside each gold work; airport codes replaced with names and lease years; the bottom timeline became one thread, with the privately operated Ideal X marked in cream rather than agency gold.

Airport coordinates: frozen OurAirports-derived DataHub table https://raw.githubusercontent.com/datasets/airport-codes/master/data/airport-codes.csv ; KEWR, KLGA, KJFK reference locations, not terminal entrances. Snapshot field order checked against expected New York-region longitude/latitude bounds. Airport factual membership: FY2026 budget p.65; historic lease years: official statements cited above.

Port/campus/crossing coordinates remain approximate references from public facility maps and the existing georeferenced USGS overview; sources and precision limits are attached to every entry. GWB shore-side anchors were tightened to the visible bridge ends instead of extending the schematic mark farther into Washington Heights. No surveyed-route claim is introduced.
