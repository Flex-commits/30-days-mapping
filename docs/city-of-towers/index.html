30-Day Map Challenge / Day 08 / Utopia

A City of Towers

The storeyed stelae of Aksum, Ethiopia, drawn at their true height. Aksum carved skyscrapers it never built.

The three great stelae

Stele 3 · King Ezana’s Stele20.6 m9 storeysstanding
Stele 1 · The Great Stele33 m13 storeysfallen
Stele 2 · The Rome Stele24.6 m10 storeysreturned 2005

Each stele is a single block of granite carved to look like a tall building: a false door at the base, then floor after floor of windows and the round ends of timber beams. Aksum’s palaces were built in the same style, but no building known from Aksum rose anywhere near thirteen floors. Raised around the 4th century AD and believed to mark royal tombs, the stelae show a city that existed only in stone.

Heights and weights as commonly published (Wikipedia, “Obelisk of Axum” and “King Ezana’s Stele”). The Great Stele’s 13 storeys as published; storeys on Steles 2 and 3 counted from photographs on Wikimedia Commons. Positions © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
